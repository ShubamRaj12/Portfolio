The Global Arc

The worldwide total rose for over a decade, held near its peak through the mid-2000s, then began a sustained decline as prevention-of-transmission programs scaled up.

03

Three Countries, One Pattern?

South Africa, Kenya, and Uganda each carry a large share of the global burden — and each has bent its curve downward, at different speeds and from different starting points.

Nigeria is not published as a separate row in this indicator series, so Uganda is used here in its place. "Cross-country average" is the unweighted mean across all 88 countries — it is pulled upward by a handful of high-burden countries, so most countries sit below it.

Methodology & Notes

  1. Source: UNAIDS estimates, published via the World Bank as indicator SH.HIV.0014 ("Children (0–14) living with HIV"), retrieved live through Our World in Data for this rebuild.
  2. Figures are modeled estimates, not direct case counts, and are rounded by the source to roughly two significant figures — read single-country trends as directional rather than exact.
  3. The global total (Section 1) is UNAIDS's own published aggregate. It runs higher than the sum of the 88 countries shown individually here, since it also folds in countries with too little data to publish separately.
  4. This project was originally built as an R / Shiny and Tableau assignment for graduate coursework; it's rebuilt here as a standalone interactive page pulling the same public indicator live, since the original dataset and output weren't preserved.
